Jérémy Carteret spent almost ten years at Benjamin Leroux in Beaune, in the last years as his right hand. In 2019 he also made his first wine of his own. In 2022 he went out on his own, with his own cellar in La Rochepot, a village in the hills behind Meursault. That first year he made around 2,000 bottles, a year later already 10,000.

Some of the grapes come from vineyards he rents and works himself, such as Les Grandes Carelles and Les Nazarettes in Pommard, planted in 1937 and 1939. The rest he buys from growers who farm organically. The grapes for Euphoria, for example, come from Les Crucibées, the vineyard of Nina Saulnier of [Les Monts](/en/producers/les-monts/).

In the vineyard, everything is about concentration. Jérémy keeps yields low, sometimes only 15 hectolitres per hectare, around six bunches per vine. Even on the oldest vines he still removes bunches in summer. He lets the shoots grow up to two metres high and, instead of cutting them, braids them together.

In the cellar he works calmly and precisely. The reds ferment with whole bunches, and he crushes the white grapes by foot. Everything ages in wooden barrels, sometimes also in a clay jar. He only adds sulphur when needed, and has his wines checked in a lab regularly for faults. A ripe year doesn't scare him, as long as the wine stays in balance.
