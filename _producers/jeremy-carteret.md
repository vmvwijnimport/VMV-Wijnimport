Jérémy Carteret spent almost ten years at Benjamin Leroux in Beaune, in the last years as his right hand. In 2019 he also made his first wine of his own. In 2022 he went out on his own, with his own cellar in La Rochepot, a village in the hills behind Meursault.

Three of his wines come from his own domaine: old vineyards in Pommard that he leases and works himself. Les Grandes Carelles was planted in 1937, Les Nazarettes in 1939. For the other wines, the négoce, he buys grapes from growers who farm organically. The grapes for Euphoria, for example, come from Les Crucibées, the vineyard of Nina Saulnier of [Les Monts](/en/producers/les-monts/).

In the vineyard, everything is about concentration. Jérémy keeps yields low, sometimes only 15 hectolitres per hectare, around six bunches per vine. Even on the oldest vines he still removes bunches in summer. He lets the shoots grow up to two metres high and, instead of cutting them, braids them together.

In the cellar he works calmly and precisely. The red grapes go into the vat as whole bunches. The whites he first crushes with his feet, before they go into a vertical press. The wines then age in oak barrels from Stockinger, a small family cooperage in Austria, and sometimes in a clay jar. Jérémy uses a lot of new oak, for some wines only new barrels. That works because Stockinger makes its barrels to give as little oak flavour as possible: you taste the grape and the vineyard, not the barrel. He only adds sulphur when needed. Jérémy is extremely allergic to brett, a yeast that makes wine smell of stable and leather: he picks it up immediately. That's why he has his wines checked in a lab regularly. A ripe year doesn't scare him, as long as the wine stays in balance.
