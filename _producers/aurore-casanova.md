Aurore Casanova was a professional ballet dancer for seven years before she returned to Champagne, to the vineyards her mother had planted in the 1960s. While studying viticulture and oenology in Avize, she met Jean-Baptiste Robinet, who comes from a winemaking family himself. In 2013 Aurore took over her mother's plots and together they started their estate in Mardeuil, close to Épernay. Today they farm 4.8 hectares, with grand cru plots in Puisieulx, Le Mesnil-sur-Oger and Oger, as well as vineyards in Champvoisy. They work biodynamically and are certified organic.

We have been working with Aurore and Jean-Baptiste for a while now, and what has stayed with us most is how much thought goes into every decision. Nothing happens without a reason. For example, they pick into white crates instead of dark ones, so the grapes arrive in the cellar cooler and in better condition. A small detail, but it says a lot. You recognise that precision in the wines too, and it is hard not to see the discipline of a ballet dancer in them.

In the cellar they work without a fixed recipe. In a generous year they let the terroir do the work; in a more difficult year they can draw on their réserve perpétuelle to bring the wine into balance. The question is always the same: how do you let the terroir speak? That is why they ferment with wild yeasts, only do bâtonnage when the wine asks for it and use barrels from different coopers, so you do not taste the stamp of a single cooper. The result is Champagne with depth and tension that drinks more like wine than like bubbles. The wines also work very well with a wide range of dishes.
