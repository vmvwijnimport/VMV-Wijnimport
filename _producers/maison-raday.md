Valentin Tribaut runs the family estate Champagne Tribaut Schloesser in Romery, in the Vallée de la Marne, together with his brother. In 2018 he also started Maison Raday, a small négoce using grapes from growers he admires. The name comes from the little river that flows through Romery, Cormoyeux and Fleury-la-Rivière, the villages where the Tribaut family has had vineyards for generations. The grower's name is always on the label.

He made his first cuvée in 2018 with grapes from his cousin Aurélien Lurquin, who has farmed his vineyards biodynamically since 2012. With grapes of that quality and Valentin’s talent, it was hardly a surprise that it turned out well. A year later he also started working with Régis Poissinet, a biodynamic grower in Cuchery whom he greatly admires for his work in the vineyard. From Régis’s grapes he makes the cuvée Meunier d’Ailleurs.

In the cellar Valentin does as little as possible. All the wines age in oak barrels. In 2021 mildew destroyed so much of the crop that he got no grapes at all, so he left the 2020 in barrel for 24 months instead of the usual 12. For the ageing in bottle he switched from crown caps to cork. Production is small, and Valentin prefers to stay out of the spotlight. He has no website and no Instagram. The wines have a character of their own, but above all they show the area they come from.

The first time we tasted Valentin’s wine, it was poured for us blind. We were so impressed that we contacted him straight away. After tasting in his cellar we were sure, and we’re proud to be working with him. In our view, Valentin is one of the rising names in Champagne.
