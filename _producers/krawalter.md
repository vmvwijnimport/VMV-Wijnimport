Felix Walter grew up in Bürgstadt, in Franconia, on the family estate Weingut Josef Walter. His father Christoph has been making Burgundy-inspired Spätburgunder there for years. Felix learned the craft at Künstler in the Rheingau and with Julian Huber in Baden, among others. There he fell under the spell of Burgundian Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, and decided to make wines of his own, different from those of the family estate. Krawalter was his nickname during his training, because he was always asking critical questions.

He then spent some time abroad, working at Hans Herzog in New Zealand, the Grabenwerkstatt in the Wachau, Mac Forbes in Australia, Bodega Chacra in Patagonia, Chanterêves in Burgundy and Laherte Frères in Champagne. In 2022 he made his first wine of his own at his parents’ estate. The grapes come from the Erste Lagen in Bürgstadt. The wines ferment spontaneously and are not filtered. He makes Chardonnay, Spätburgunder and a Blanc de Noirs without dosage.

We found Felix through Chanterêves, who posted glowingly about his wines on Instagram. When we tasted the wines, we were immediately enthusiastic. They are certainly Burgundian in style, but also have a character all of their own. You can clearly taste Huber’s influence: the whites have a lovely touch of reduction.
