Nina Saulnier makes wine together with Lucas Mouchonnat in Fussey, in the Hautes-Côtes de Beaune. It’s the last village in the appellation, right on the border between the Côte de Nuits and the Côte de Beaune, a few minutes' drive from Pernand-Vergelesses. Nina planted Pinot Noir and Chardonnay there on Les Crucibées, a 5.5-hectare plot that had been pasture for more than a hundred years. The plot is a monopole: it belongs entirely to her. It lies at 415 to 470 metres above sea level. The subsoil consists of marl, limestone and dolomite, with a thin layer of clay on top. They farm organically and work hard to support biodiversity in and around the vineyard.

The grapes from Les Crucibées are used for Jérémy Carteret's cuvée Euphoria. When Nina tasted what this young vineyard could produce, she decided to make her own wine from it too. 2023 is the first vintage, with a white and a red Les Crucibées. In the cellar they add nothing. The wines are lightly filtered and only get a little sulphur when needed. Thanks to the altitude and the cool site, the wines are fresh and precise.

Nina and Lucas are also working on a new vineyard: Sous Châtelet, planted with Aligoté. You may already know the name: Lalou Bize-Leroy makes the Aligoté of Domaine d'Auvenay from this vineyard. In the cellar, Nina and Lucas are also experimenting with clay, steel and wooden barrels of different sizes.
